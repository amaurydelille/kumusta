# Beginner lessons: native-review notes

Points a native Metro Manila speaker should double-check, by lesson.

## 1. Greet your neighbour politely

- "Mabuti naman po, salamat po." has two po. Is that natural, or does it sound over-polite? An
  alternative is "Okay lang po, salamat."
- "Uy! Kumusta ka?" is fine for a peer. Check that "Uy" reads as friendly and not abrupt.

## 2. Introduce yourself at a barkada party

- "Hi! Ako si Sam." Would a native speaker say "Ako si Sam" or "Sam nga pala"? At a party, people
  also often introduce themselves in English.
- "Ikaw, anong pangalan mo?" uses the spelling "anong", not "ano'ng". We use "anong" in every
  lesson.
- "Taga-Canada ako, pero sa Makati ako nakatira." Check that the word order sounds natural (compare
  "nakatira ako sa Makati").
- "Kita-kits" is slang that can feel dated (2000s). Does it still sound natural, or should it be
  "Sige, kita tayo mamaya!"?

## 3. Meet Lola with a mano

- "Mano po, Lola." We translated it as "Your blessing, Lola." Is that acceptable?
- "ang boyfriend ko" is Taglish. We chose it over "nobyo", which sounds formal or old-fashioned to
  many young Manila speakers. Please confirm.
- "Kakain na po kami." Check where po sits among the enclitics (na po vs po na).

## 4. Order at the carinderia

- "Saka isang kanin po." We used the standard spelling "saka". Most Manila speakers say and write
  "tsaka". Pick one.
- "Isa nga pong sinigang." Would a native speaker say "Isang sinigang nga po" instead?
- "Heto po ang bayad ko." "Eto po bayad" is more common in fast speech.

## 5. Sari-sari store run

- Spanish numbers: "otsenta" (80) and "bente" (20). For the bill we used "isang daan" (100), not
  "siyento" or "one hundred". Is that how a sari-sari owner and a customer would say it? Many people
  use English numbers for 100.
- "isang daan" vs "isandaan" vs "sandaan": we used the two-word spelling.
- "May malamig po ba kayong tubig?" Is that natural, or is "May malamig na tubig po kayo?" more
  common?
- "Bente po ang sukli, di ba?" Check that "di ba" with po isn't too casual for an older store owner.

## 6. Let's eat: lunch with a friend

- "Ako, gusto ko ng sisig." Check the topic-fronted "Ako," and whether "Sisig na lang ako" sounds
  more native.
- "Sa kanto na lang tayo, mura doon." Should "doon" be "dun" (casual) or "roon" (strict
  din/rin-style rule after a vowel)? We kept "doon".

## 7. Ride a jeepney

- "Kuya, Cubao po ba ito?" Is it natural to check the route this way, or is "Dadaan po ba 'to ng
  Cubao?" better?
- "Kuya, bayad daw po." (passing on another passenger's fare): please confirm this is what people
  actually say. Some say just "Bayad po" for any fare.
- "Pakisabi po kapag Cubao na." Is "Paki-sabi lang po pag Cubao na" more natural?

## 8. Ask for directions

- "may ATM po ba malapit dito?" The strict rule is "rito" after a vowel, but "dito" follows a
  consonant here, so it's fine. Is the word order natural (compare "May ATM po ba dito malapit?")?
- "Diretso" vs "deretso": both spellings are common. We used "diretso".
- "botika" vs "drugstore": both are used in Manila, and "drugstore" may be more common among younger
  speakers.
- "Kaya bang lakarin?" We believe this is natural. Please confirm.

## 9. Halo-halo and fruit at the market

- "Puwede" vs "pwede": we used the standard "puwede" (here and in lesson 10). Most people text
  "pwede".
- "Puwede pong dagdagan ng leche flan?" Is "Pa-extra po ng leche flan" more natural Manila Taglish?
- "May tawad pa po?" Check this against "Wala na po bang tawad?" or "Pwede pong tumawad?"
- "Isang daan na lang po, kukunin ko na." Check the price realism for a kilo of mangoes.

## 10. Set a time to meet up

- "alas-tres ng hapon": the Spanish hour plus "ng hapon". Please confirm.
- "Anong oras ka puwede?" We think this is natural, but compare "Anong oras ka free?"
- "Huwag kang male-late, ha!" is Taglish ("male-late"), and we spelled it with a hyphen. Native
  speakers also say "Wag kang ma-late" or "Huwag kang mahuhuli". Is "Huwag" too formal compared with
  "Wag"?
- "Kita tayo sa harap ng simbahan." Please confirm this is natural.
