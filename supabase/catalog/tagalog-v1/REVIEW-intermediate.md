# Intermediate Tagalog: native-review notes

Things a native Metro Manila speaker should double-check, per lesson.

## 1. Sunday lunch at Lola's

- "Ang bango po dito, Lola!": "dito" vs "rito" after a vowel (po). Spoken Manila uses "dito";
  confirm we prefer that over the strict d/r rule.
- "Pabalot na lang po ako, para may baon ako bukas.": is "pabalot" natural for asking Lola to pack
  leftovers, or would "Pa-take home na lang po" / "Ipagbalot n'yo na lang po ako" sound more
  natural?
- "Sige po, pero konting kanin lang.": "konting" vs "kaunting". We used the spoken form.

## 2. Sound natural with little words

- "Ang tagal na nating hindi nagkita.": check that the word order sounds natural (vs "Ang tagal
  nating hindi nagkita").
- "Na-late ako kanina kasi grabe ang traffic sa EDSA.": Taglish "na-late" spelling with the hyphen.
- "Ikakasal na raw si Carlo": raw after "na" follows the rule; confirm it doesn't sound overly
  careful (many speakers say "daw" everywhere).

## 3. Weekend stories

- "Kaninang umaga, naglaba ako at naglinis ng bahay.": a second "ako" is dropped before "naglinis".
  Check that it sounds fine.
- "Aalis kami mga alas-siyete.": "alas-siyete" spelling (vs "alas-syete"). We used "mga" rather than
  "nang/ng" before the time to avoid the ng/nang question.

## 4. Ask someone out to the movies

- "Kita tayo sa Megamall mga alas-sais, okay lang ba?": the casual "kita tayo" instead of "magkita
  tayo".
- "Dalawang ticket po para sa alas-siyete y medya.": is the Spanish time natural at a cinema
  counter, or would people say "seven-thirty"? Also check "medya" vs "media".
- "Pwede po bang magkatabi kami, sa bandang gitna?": check that "magkatabi kami" works without a
  verb like "umupo".

## 5. Island trip to Puerto Galera

- "Ang mahal naman po. Pwede bang singkwenta na lang?": fifty pesos is unrealistically low for a
  tricycle to White Beach. Adjust the amount (for example "dos siyentos" or "isandaan") if you want
  it realistic, and check which number style drivers actually use.
- "may booking po ako sa pangalang Alex Smith": "sa pangalang" vs a Taglish "under Alex Smith".
  Which do front desks hear more?
- "biyahe" spelling (vs "byahe").

## 6. At the doctor and the pharmacy

- "tatlong araw na po akong may lagnat": check the enclitic order and the "akong may" construction.
- "at inuubo ako": vs "inuubo rin ako" or "may ubo rin ako".
- "Ilang beses ko po ba ito iinumin sa isang araw?": "iinumin" for tablets is standard; confirm "sa
  isang araw" (vs "isang araw") for "a day".

## 7. Renting a condo

- "upa" vs plain English "rent": many Manila renters say "rent". We kept "upa" but noted the English
  option.
- "Kung pipirma po ako ng isang taon": "pumirma ng isang taon" for "sign for a year" may sound
  translated. Alternative: "Kung isang taon po ang kontrata".
- "pwede bang bawasan nang kaunti?": the use of "nang", and whether "tawaran" or "babaan" sounds
  more natural.

## 8. Office small talk in Taglish

- Check the overall Taglish mix in a BGC office: "ang dami kong deadlines this week", "i-send ko na
  lang sa'yo yung file after lunch".
- "sa'yo" spelling (vs "sa 'yo" or "sa iyo").
- "mamayang alas-dos": would offices say "mamayang 2" / "mamayang two" instead?
- "sabay na tayong mag-lunch mamaya": check it's natural.

## 9. Christmas in the Philippines

- "alas-kuwatro magsisimula ang Simbang Gabi": "alas-kuwatro" vs "alas-kwatro". Check the fronted
  time.
- "mag-Noche Buena na tayo": check the Taglish-style "mag-" on a Spanish noun. Alternative: "Noche
  Buena na!"
- "Naku, nag-abala pa po kayo.": the standard gift-receiving phrase. Confirm.

## 10. Wrong order at the restaurant

- "in-order" spelling (vs "inorder" or "inorder ko"). We used a hyphen to match "i-send" and
  "na-late".
- "Kalahating oras na po kaming naghihintay.": check it sounds firm but not rude.
- "Pakitanggal na lang po yung hipon sa bill.": "pakitanggal" vs Taglish "paki-remove".
